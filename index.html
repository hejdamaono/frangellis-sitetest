A South Philly tradition

Some things
shouldn't change.

Frangelli's Bakery began in 1947 when Joseph Frangelli opened a bakery at 9th & Jackson in South Philadelphia.

The bakery moved a few blocks south to 9th & Ritner in 1994, where Frangelli's continues its neighborhood tradition today.

The goal has stayed simple: traditional recipes, fresh baking, and something worth coming back for.

The old-fashioned way

Every doughnut
is one at a time.

Frangelli's says every doughnut is hand cut and every doughnut is hand pumped to order. It's a small detail, but it's the kind of detail that makes a neighborhood bakery feel different.

From the people behind the counter
“I was making the cheese for the cannoli. I had it in the bowl from out of the mixer and I was hungry, so I ripped a piece of doughnut off, put the cheese on it, and went, ‘Wow. Really good.’”

— John Colosi, describing the origin of the Franolli

Colosi bought Frangelli's in 2010 after learning the bakery's methods from Tony Frangelli and longtime bakers. His story is a good example of what makes the bakery interesting: the tradition stays intact while new ideas occasionally make their way into the case.
